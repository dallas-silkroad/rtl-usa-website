Porch piracy & secure delivery

Porch piracy, answered: the problem, the patents, the path to license.

Plain-language answers for carriers, smart-home platforms, homebuilders, retailers, and anyone else who has been asked "why has nobody fixed this yet?"

The problem
What is porch piracy?

Porch piracy is the theft of delivered packages left unattended at a doorstep, porch, or lobby before the recipient retrieves them. It is a last-mile problem: the package has been delivered correctly, but nothing secures it between the driver's hand and the homeowner's.

Industry estimates put the scale at more than 120 million packages stolen from U.S. doorsteps a year, roughly $12 billion in annual losses across consumers and industry, and about one in four U.S. households reporting a stolen package within a year.

Who actually pays for porch piracy?

Everyone in the delivery chain, which is why nobody has owned the fix. Carriers absorb claims and re-delivery costs. Retailers eat the re-ship, the refund, and often the lost repeat customer. Homeowners lose trust in the whole delivery experience and shift purchases to in-store pickup. Insurers, property managers, and homebuilders feel it indirectly through claims and complaints.

Why haven't doorbell cameras and parcel lockers solved it?

Because neither one verifies and secures the package at the door. A doorbell camera records the theft after the fact. A parcel locker moves the drop-off somewhere other than the home, which adds a trip for the customer and does not scale to every doorstep. In-home access systems ask the homeowner to let a stranger inside.

The missing step is a verified, locked hand-off at the home itself: the package is scanned, passes through a secured opening, and the recipient is notified with proof. That step is what RTL's patents cover.

RTL's technology
What does RTL's secure delivery technology do?

It turns the doorstep into a verified, locked delivery point. The driver presents the package to a scanner built into a secure cabinet or wall-mounted vault. The barcode is read automatically, a one-time access door opens, the package passes into a locked interior compartment, and the recipient receives a verified confirmation with the captured package data on their phone. Optional features in the portfolio include motion-activated camera capture, tamper alarms, biometric access, and blockchain-encrypted delivery records.

The short version on the site is Scan. Drop. Secure.

What is the difference between the Built-In and Stand-Alone models?

Built-In is the OEM model: a delivery vault embedded into door systems, walls, and smart-home platforms, designed for new construction and ecosystem partners such as smart-home brands and production homebuilders.

Stand-Alone is the retrofit model: an add-on unit for the roughly 140 million existing U.S. homes, installed without construction and working with every carrier's current workflow. Both models share the same patented core.

Does it work with USPS, FedEx, UPS, and Amazon deliveries?

Yes. The technology is carrier-agnostic. The Stand-Alone unit needs no carrier integration at all: the driver scans the package label as they would today and the unit handles verification and secure drop-off. The Built-In model can additionally push branded notifications through a carrier's or smart-home platform's own app, which is where carriers can offer a premium verified-delivery tier.

The patents
What do RTL's patents cover?

RTL holds two issued U.S. patents with priority back to a provisional filed February 10, 2018. U.S. Patent 10,954,068 covers a pass-through chute with exterior and interior doors, an internal conveyor, an in-chute scanner that reads the package code, and a control system that notifies the recipient. U.S. Patent 11,812,878 covers an exterior-anchored cabinet with interlocked doors, automatic scanning as the package enters, and automatic verified confirmation with captured package data sent to a mobile device.

These are plain-language summaries for discussion, not the claim language and not a legal opinion. Detailed claim charts are available under NDA.
