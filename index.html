The claims — what a competitor must infringe

You're not licensing an idea. You're licensing the claim.

Cameras record the theft and lockers sit apart from the home — neither verifies the package at the door. RTL's independent claims sit exactly on the step every existing product skips.

U.S. 10,954,068 · Claim 1, summarized

A chute through the building wall with exterior and interior doors, a conveyor that carries the package inward, a scanner inside the chute that reads the package code, and a control system that notifies the recipient — delivery into a secured interior cabinet. To compete, a rival needs scan-verified pass-through delivery into the structure.

U.S. 11,812,878 · Claim 1, summarized

An exterior-anchored cabinet whose interior door closes whenever the exterior door is open, an unlocked first door with a scanner that auto-reads the code as the package passes, and automatic verified confirmation with captured package data to a mobile device. To compete, a rival needs auto-scan on entry, interlocked doors, and automatic verified confirmation.

U.S. 12,750,465 · Claim 1, summarized

A camera housing mounted on top of a mailbox, with its own power and processing, that starts recording when activity enters a pre-set zone around the mailbox (centered on the camera and covering the roadside in front of the home, plus the area in front of and behind the box), and that networks with the home's alarm system, garage door opener and doorbell so that any of them can trigger the mailbox camera. To compete, a rival needs a mailbox-mounted camera with a zone-based recording trigger that is linked to the home's other security devices.

U.S. 12,750,465 · Claim 9, summarized

The complete system: a mailbox with a box body sized to receive both mail and packages and an access door, paired with the same top-mounted, zone-triggered camera device networked to the home's alarm, garage opener and doorbell. This claim covers the mailbox and the security device sold together as one product.

Depth behind claim 1

Dependent claims extend to blockchain-encrypted records ('878 cl. 4; '465 cl. 7, 15), motion-activated camera capture ('878 cl. 6; '465 cl. 3, 11), biometric access ('878 cl. 8), tamper alarms ('878 cl. 7), in-cabinet conveyor ('878 cl. 10), and locked access door with padded interior ('068 cl. 2 to 3). The '465 patent adds a 360-degree camera (cl. 2, 10), battery and solar-strip power on the mailbox top (cl. 4, 12), wireless video transfer to the homeowner's device and the cloud (cl. 5, 13), AI person recognition that triggers recording (cl. 6, 14), and a recording zone set by radius or by geofence aligned to the property line (cl. 8, 16, 17). Priority for the '068 and '878 patents runs to a provisional filed February 10, 2018. Detailed claim charts available under NDA.
